## Where AI saves you time without crossing a line

| Task | OK? | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drafting a brief, instructions or an email to students | Yes | Check it, and say how you used it. [[rule:t-3a]] |
| Generating exam or quiz questions from your slides | Yes | Review each for correctness and bias. Weak questions are common. |
| Drafting a rubric from your learning outcomes | Yes | Before any work is graded. [[rule:t-1]] |
| Polishing your comments after you've set the grade | Yes | Language only, no new substance. [[rule:t-3b]] |
| Turning your own notes into feedback sentences on ungraded work | Yes | The judgement must be yours. Disclose. On graded work, only language edits after the grade is set. [[rule:t-3b]] |
| Checking whether the references in a student's list exist | Yes | Paste only the reference list, no name. Confirm every flagged reference yourself. See below. |
| Letting AI write the feedback, which you lightly edit | No | Not your feedback. Only as a clearly announced experiment. |
| Asking AI what grade a submission deserves | No | Not even as a second opinion. [[rule:t-3b]] |
| Pasting unpublished theses or health information into a free tool | No | Sensitive data stays out of unapproved tools. [[rule:t-4]] |

:::tip Checking reference lists
Running a reference list through a chatbot with web search is a quick way to catch invented references, and we encourage it. It's a routine procedural check, the kind the [[EU AI Act]] doesn't treat as high-risk. Keep it apart from grading: you're checking whether sources exist, not judging the work, and you confirm each flagged reference by hand before it affects anything. It won't catch students with paid or agentic tools, which rarely invent references any more.
:::

:::grey Does this square with clause 3B?
Clause 3B says AI must not influence a grade "directly or indirectly", and a fake reference you find this way will matter for the assessment. Our reading: the AI only points to where to look; the finding is yours, from your own lookup. The next version of the policy should say this explicitly. [[rule:t-3b]]
:::

:::beginner Which tool may I use?
**Microsoft 365 [[Copilot]] Chat**, signed in with your SU account, is in practice the only tool SU has approved. Staff have it, and students most likely do too through their SU Microsoft 365 account. Your data isn't used to train the model. It's a good place to start if you're new to this or unsure about data safety. It's not a requirement: ChatGPT, Claude and others are often stronger, and fine for work that contains no student data or other sensitive information.

Two limits apply whatever you use. Even Copilot Chat isn't cleared for information at confidentiality level 3 or 4, because chat history is saved. And free tools outside the university login have no data protection. Before you paste student work into anything, check with the SRC staff responsible for [[GDPR]]. See [[approved tool]] and SU's [AI resources page](https://medarbetare.su.se/en/support-and-service/it/ai-resources-at-stockholm-university).
:::

## Say how you used it, in one sentence

Students trust your feedback more when they know what AI did and didn't do. The policy asks for a short disclosure: which tool, and for what ([[rule:t-transparency|Transparency matters]]). Build yours here; it goes into your kit.
