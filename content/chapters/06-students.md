## Say it on day one

Students can only follow rules they've been told. And they can't be fairly failed for breaking rules nobody showed them. So the first message is simple: **have your AI rules ready before the course starts, and go through them in the first session.**

Here's a five-minute script built from your course and the SRC rules. Adapt it to suit your needs.

<div data-widget="kit" data-part="lecture"></div>

:::tip Point them to both policies
Link the student policy *and* the teacher policy in the course room, and ask students to read both before the first assignment. If you've adapted the rules for your course, put your version where students will find it, next to the SRC policies.
:::

:::tip Repeat it where it matters
Put the rule for each assignment in the brief itself, not just the syllabus. Students read briefs; they skim syllabi.
:::

:::tip Let them meet the rules before it counts
Give a short ungraded practice task early, with the same AI rules as the graded work. Misunderstandings show up there, where you can explain and move on, instead of in graded work, where you'd have to report them (chapter 7).
:::

## Teach disclosure, with examples

Students are asked to disclose meaningful AI use: which tool, for what, and how it changed the work ([[rule:s-2|student policy 2]]). Most have never written one. Show them the difference:

| Too vague | Useful |
| --- | --- |
| "AI was used in this assignment." | "I used ChatGPT to explain the difference between resilience and robustness, then checked it against Folke (2006). I used Copilot to check grammar in the final draft." |
| "I used AI for help." | "I asked Claude for counterarguments to my main claim. I added one of them (section 3) and rejected the others." |
| (nothing) | "I didn't use AI for this assignment." |

Also say plainly: **disclosing a use that isn't allowed doesn't make it allowed.** And disclosing allowed use never lowers the grade. Some students hide use because they fear exactly that.

## Show them how to use it to learn

The SRC rules allow AI as a tutor. Most students don't know what that looks like and use it as a ghostwriter by default. Give them a few prompts:

- *"Quiz me on the main concepts in this text, one question at a time. Don't give the answer until I've tried."*
- *"Explain [concept] in simple terms, then ask me to explain it back and tell me what I got wrong."*
- *"Here's my argument. Give me the three strongest objections a critical reader would raise."*
- *"Here's my draft. Don't rewrite it. Point out where the argument is weakest and why."*
- *"What would a researcher from [another discipline] say is missing from this analysis?"*

### Built-in learning modes

The big chatbots now have a mode that tutors instead of answering: it asks questions, gives hints and checks understanding. Worth showing in class, for a minute each:

- **ChatGPT: study mode.** Switch it on from the **+** (tools) menu in the message box. [OpenAI's guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11780217)
- **Claude: the Learning skill.** Add it under *Customize → Skills*, then switch it on in a chat. [Anthropic's guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10181068-styles-are-moving-to-skills)
- **Microsoft Copilot: the Study and Learn agent**, in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app, if the account has it. It also makes flashcards and quizzes from uploaded material. [Microsoft's guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/education/copilot/getting-started-study-learn-agent)
- **Gemini: Guided Learning.** Under *More tools*, but at the time of writing only in the US, for adults, and not with school accounts. [Google's guide](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16448384)

Menus move around. If one isn't where the guide says, a search for the mode's name usually finds the current steps. Checked November 2026.

:::grey The line between tutor and ghostwriter is fuzzy
"Point out the weakest argument" can slide into "fix it for me" over a few messages. That's why the [[process evidence|process-evidence]] and conversations from chapter 5 matter more than any rule wording.
:::
