## Your Course AI Kit

The SRC rules made concrete for your course, with practical tips, exercises and your first-lecture script. Adapt it to suit your needs; it's yours.

===

## Before the course starts

- [ ] AI rules in the syllabus, and the relevant one in every assignment brief.
- [ ] Both SRC policies, the [[rule:s-scope|student policy]] and the [[rule:t-scope|teacher policy]], linked in the course room on Athena, or your adapted course rules if you have them. Students asked to read them before the first assignment.
- [ ] Five minutes in the first lecture to go through the rules and the *why*.
- [ ] Students told how to disclose, with an example.
- [ ] A short ungraded practice task early on, so students meet the rules before it counts.
- [ ] The in-class or oral parts of the assessment in the schedule (chapter 5), and in the syllabus.
- [ ] A plan for students with approved study adaptations.
- [ ] Same rules agreed with co-teachers and TAs.

## Keep up

The SRC rules will change as tools change. The **Rules** button always shows the current version, and you'll see a notice here when it changes. When this course gets an update, it's listed on the [about page](#/p/about).

===

## Three things to take with you

1. **Under our rules, unauthorised AI use is hard to spot.** SRC rules out detectors for now (they'd be high-risk use under the EU AI Act), and good AI text doesn't look like AI. Don't build your course on catching it.
2. **So examine the important part in the room.** In-class writing, in-class exams, oral checks, seminars where students explain their own work. Let take-home work be practice and preparation.
3. **Keep the SRC rules, make them concrete, and say them on day one.** Adapt them only if you know these tools well and have a reason.
