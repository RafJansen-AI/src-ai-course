## The rules on one screen

SRC has two AI policies that are meant to be read together: one for **teaching staff** and one for **students**. You can open both with the **Rules** button at any point. The short version for you as a teacher:

1. **Use AI to support your teaching, not to replace your judgement.** Drafting instructions, examples, rubrics and exam questions is fine. Supervision, approvals and assessment decisions stay yours. [[rule:t-1]]
2. **You're responsible for everything you send students, and you say how you used AI.** One sentence is enough: which tool, what for. [[rule:t-2]]
3. **AI stays out of grading.** It can't propose, justify or influence a grade, not even as a second opinion. Language polish of your comments *after* the grade is decided is fine. And no [[AI detectors]] for now: SRC treats them as high-risk use under the [[EU AI Act]]. [[rule:t-3b]] [[rule:t-3c]]
4. **Sensitive student data stays out of unapproved tools.** Health information, disciplinary matters, unpublished theses. [[rule:t-4]]

The teacher policy applies to everyone in the same way; it isn't something you adapt for your course. The policy's own picture is useful: treat AI as a colleague who is *knowledgeable, fast and overconfident, but not accountable* for your students, your course or your grades.

:::beginner What's the difference between the policy and my course rules?
The teacher policy is fixed: it applies to all teaching staff. The student policy is the default for every course. Course coordinators may adapt the student rules for a course or assignment, but most courses should keep them and make them concrete (chapter 4).
:::

## And the student side

You'll set the rules for your own course in the next chapter, but you need to know the default your students start from. The [[rule:s-1|student policy]] in brief:

- Students may use AI to **support their learning**: explaining concepts, discussion, feedback on their own work, language help.
- AI must **not substitute for the work being assessed**. What they hand in must "primarily represent their own intellectual contribution". [[rule:s-1]]
- Where AI meaningfully influenced the work, students **[[disclose|disclosure]] it**: which tool, for what, how it changed the work. [[rule:s-2]]
- **Disclosure doesn't make a forbidden use OK.** Saying "I had ChatGPT write this" is honest, but still not allowed. [[rule:s-2]]
- Breaking the policy or your course rules can be **academic misconduct**, handled through SU's usual procedure. [[rule:s-3]]
- A course **may adapt the default**, stricter or looser, and students must follow what the course says. [[rule:s-4]]

:::grey
"Primarily their own intellectual contribution" is a judgement call, and students will read it more generously than you do. That's why the next chapter has you spell out what the rules mean for each of your assignments.
:::

:::tip Point students to both policies
Students should read the student policy *and* the teacher policy, so they know what to expect from you too. Link both in the course room, or your adapted course rules if you have them. Chapter 6 covers how to present them on day one.
:::
